INT. APARTMENT LIVING ROOM — AFTERNOON SUNLIGHT

Dust motes drift through warm golden light. A leather-bound notebook lies open on a glass coffee table, pages filled with hurried handwriting.

SAMAR (22) holds an envelope with an unopened seal. He turns it over three times before looking at MEERA across the room.

SAMAR
If you write something down, does that make it more real? Or does it just mean you can't pretend it never happened?

---page---

MEERA doesn't look away from the window. The sound of distant traffic hums below.

MEERA
Ink doesn't lie, Samar. People do when they read it out loud.

He slides the notebook closer. The handwriting is uneven — some paragraphs crossed out with deliberate, heavy strokes.

SAMAR
Then let's read it together. Page by page.

CUT TO:

---page---

EXT. CAMPUS CORRIDOR — FLASHBACK

Rushing students, festival posters lining the brick walls. TAMASHA banner hanging from the balcony above.

SAMAR watches MEERA carry a stack of event registrations. She drops one. He picks it up before it touches the wet ground.

SAMAR
You're running Band Baaja Baaraat on pure caffeine, aren't you?

MEERA
Caffeine and eighteen hundred people expecting the best night of their semester.

---page---

She takes the flyer, smiles, and runs ahead. Samar watches her disappear into the bustling crowd.

Back in the present, Samar closes the leather notebook. A small smile touches his face.

SAMAR
(softly)
Some notes stay with you forever.

DISSOLVE TO BLACK.
